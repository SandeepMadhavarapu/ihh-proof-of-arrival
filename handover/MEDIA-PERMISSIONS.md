# Interview & Media Permissions Directory

Where an inductee's own voice or a primary document is held, who holds the rights, and how to ask.
Researched 24 October 2026. Every rights quotation below is an exact span from the holder's own page,
re-checked by `review/workstream-3/check_media_permissions.py` (pages read with the polite fetcher)
and `review/workstream-3/browser-span-check.json` (pages read in an ordinary browser tab).
Nobody was contacted and nothing was submitted. The machine-readable version is
[`media-permissions.csv`](media-permissions.csv); a draft request letter (never sent) is in
[`PERMISSION-REQUEST-TEMPLATE.md`](PERMISSION-REQUEST-TEMPLATE.md).

"Not found" means not found in the sources checked, not proof that nothing exists.

## Albright

**1. Miller Center oral history (Clinton Presidential History Project)**
- Item: https://millercenter.org/the-presidency/presidential-oral-histories/madeleine-k-albright-oral-history. The page says "Interview Date(s) August 30, 2006" and "This is the Madeleine Albright interview as a part of the Clinton Presidential History Project."
- Rights: the only statement on the page is the site notice "© Copyright 2026. Rector and Visitors of the University of Virginia". There is no item-level rights statement, so treat it as copyrighted.
- Route: https://millercenter.org/contact ("P.O. Box 400406 Charlottesville, VA 22904").
- Ask for: permission to quote a short excerpt with credit, and whether audio exists.
- Confidence: medium (the rights wording is the site footer).

**2. Clinton Library, Dayton History Project interview**
- Item: https://clinton.presidentiallibraries.us/items/show/12627, "1996-10-28, Dayton History Project Interview with Madeleine Albright October 28, 1996" (Collection: Bosnian Declassified Records).
- Rights: not stated on the holder page.
- Route: https://www.clintonlibrary.gov/research ("Learn more about reproductions, requests, and access to the av collections.").
- Ask for: confirmation of status before quoting.

## Berlin

**1. NYPL, Irving Berlin collection of non-commercial sound recordings**
- Item: https://archives.nypl.org/rha/20489. "This collection contains non-commercial recordings of Irving Berlin and his music. The recordings are from radio broadcasts, live performances, and private recordings."
- Rights: "No copying of private, non-commercial material is allowed without the written permission of the proprietary rights holder."
- Route: "For further information contact the Curator, The Rodgers and Hammerstein Archives of Recorded Sound."
- Confidence: medium. The page does not say which items have Berlin speaking or singing himself.

**2. *Irving Sings Berlin* (Koch International Classics, 2001)**
- Item: https://www.nypl.org/research/research-catalog/bib/b16425702 ("1 sound disc (53:40 min.)", "Library of Congress songwriter series").
- Rights: a commercial release, so the rights sit with the label and the song publisher. Not stated on the catalog page.
- Song licensing: the estate (https://www.irvingberlin.com/licensing-rentals) says "To use an Irving Berlin song for advertising, TV, films, video games, etc., please contact: Universal Music Publishing".

## Cruz

## Einstein

**1. AIP, two recordings of Einstein speaking**
- Items:
  - https://history.aip.org/catalog/icos/24126.html, "Mein glaubensbekenntnis [sound recording] / 1932.";
  - https://history.aip.org/catalog/icos/24147.html, "Dialog on the present war and the future of Europe [sound recording] / 1940."
- Rights: not stated in what could be read. The full catalog records sit behind a bot-verification page, which was not bypassed.
- Literary rights: Hebrew University (https://en.huji.ac.il/einstein). "In 1982, the Estate of Albert Einstein transferred all the literary rights to Einstein's writings to the Hebrew University of Jerusalem" and "In 1991, the Executive of the Hebrew University of Jerusalem decided to transfer responsibility for all copyright issues pertaining to Albert Einstein to the office of the Curator of the Albert Einstein Archives in Jerusalem."
- Route: AIP for access, the Einstein Archives curator for permission. albert-einstein.org could not be retrieved, because its robots.txt was unreachable.

**2. Declaration of Intention, 15 Jan 1936 (PUBLIC DOMAIN)**
- Item: https://docsteach.org/documents/document/declaration-intention-einstein ("Declaration of Intention for Albert Einstein; 1/15/1936").
- Evidence: "Rights: Public Domain, Free of Known Copyright Restrictions."
- Why: the holder (National Archives, via DocsTeach) states it. It is a US court record.
- Credit: "National Archives at New York, Records of District Courts of the United States, Record Group 21."

## Hope

**Library of Congress, *The Pepsodent Show***
- Item: https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/bobhope/radio.html. "Recording of The Pepsodent Show, February 3, 1948. Lacquer disk. Bob Hope Collection, Motion Picture, Broadcasting and Recorded Sound Division, Library of Congress".
- Rights: not stated on that page. The Library's general position (https://www.loc.gov/legal/) is "As a publicly supported institution, we generally do not own the rights to materials in our collections." and "Transmission or reproduction of protected items beyond what is allowed by fair use or other exemptions requires written permission from the copyright holder."
- Route: https://ask.loc.gov/. Ask who holds rights in the broadcast.
- Image rights: the Bob & Dolores Hope Foundation (https://bobhope.com/contact) says "For information related to the use of Bob Hope’s image, read our Terms of Use."
- Not retrieved: the Library's Recorded Sound research-center pages, which showed a bot-verification page.

## Lamarr

**1. Interview tapes (privately held)**
- Source: PBS American Masters (https://www.pbs.org/wnet/americanmasters/bombshell-hedy-lamarr-story-journalist-unearths-audio-taped-hedy-interviews/10186). "Journalist Fleming Meeks unearths never-before-heard audio tapes of Hollywood actress Hedy Lamarr" and "So in all there were four tapes".
- Holder: a private individual. No institutional holder or published contact was found.
- Recommendation: do not approach an individual from this directory. Ask PBS or the film's distributor.
- Confidence: low.

**2. NARA file "M-92 Hedwig Loder (Née Lamarr)" (UNRESTRICTED)**
- Item: https://catalog.archives.gov/id/74395327
- Evidence: "Access: Unrestricted , Use: Unrestricted". This is a US federal record (Record Group 260).
- Credit: "National Archives, Record Group 260."

**3. U.S. Patent 2,292,387 (1942) (FEDERAL, NO COPYRIGHT RESTRICTION)**
- Item: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/2292387 (a scanned PDF)
- Evidence: USPTO terms (https://www.uspto.gov/terms-use-uspto-websites): "Subject to limited exceptions reflected in 37 CFR 1.71(d) & (e) and 1.84(s), the text and drawings of a patent are typically not subject to copyright restrictions." The USPTO also asks "that in any subsequent use the United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) be given appropriate acknowledgement".
- Credit: "Source: United States Patent and Trademark Office."

## Muir

**No recording of Muir's voice was found.**
- Evidence: Melville B. Anderson, "The Conversation of John Muir" (https://scholarlycommons.pacific.edu/jmr-all/56) wishes for one: "give for a phonographic record of those wonderful talks!"
- That page's rights: "Copyright Status Copyright status unknown". It also says "Please contact this institution directly to obtain copies of the images or permission to publish or use".

**Alternative: letter to his family, 19 Nov 1903**
- Item: https://calisphere.org/item/ark:/13030/kt1779r4pm/ ("Letter from John Muir to [Family], 1903 Nov 19"). The contributing institution is UC Berkeley, Bancroft Library.
- Rights: "The unpublished works of John Muir are copyrighted by the Muir-Hanna Trust."
- Route: the item page's "Contact Owner" link, which goes to the Bancroft Library. Pacific's own Holt-Atherton forms links now redirect to the general Libraries homepage.

## Naismith

**Library of Congress, *We the People*, 31 Jan 1939**
- Sources:
  - LoC blog (https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2016/03/a-voice-from-hoops-history/): "The “We the People” recordings were part of the WOR-AM collection of radio programs donated to the Library on lacquer discs in 1984." and "the recording is about 2.5 minutes long".
  - KU Libraries (https://exhibits.lib.ku.edu/exhibits/show/naismith150/collections/radio-interview): "is believed to be the only known audio recording of Naismith."
- Rights: KU holds an excerpt but says "However, usage rights do not extend to granting permission for use or to copying the audio for anyone other party." The Library of Congress does not generally own rights (quoted under Hope).
- Route: https://ask.loc.gov/
- Confidence: high for the item; the rights holder is unknown.

## Pei

**1. JFK Library oral history JFKOH-IMP-01 (PUBLIC DOMAIN)**
- Item: https://www.jfklibrary.org/asset-viewer/archives/jfkoh-imp-01. "Date(s) of Materials 18 March 2003", "Use Restriction Status None", "Copyright Status Donated to the United States".
- Why: the library's copyright page (https://www.jfklibrary.org/archives/research-support-services/copyright) explains "In other cases, the donor of the physical materials in a collection also donated copyright of protected materials within the collection, placing those items in the public domain."
- Credit, as the library asks: "I.M. Pei, recorded interview by Vicki Daitch, March 18, 2003, (page number), John F. Kennedy Library Oral History Program."
- Audio enquiries: "Audiovisual items: JFK.AVarchives@nara.gov".

**2. Immigration arrival case file (UNRESTRICTED)**
- Item: https://catalog.archives.gov/id/28882547 ("Ship of Arrival: President Coolidge, 08/28/1935")
- Evidence: "Access: Unrestricted , Use: Unrestricted". This is a US federal record.
- Ordering copies: https://www.archives.gov/research/order. NARA notes that restrictions can exist ("The most common are preservation priorities, copyright, and donor restrictions."), but this file is marked unrestricted.
