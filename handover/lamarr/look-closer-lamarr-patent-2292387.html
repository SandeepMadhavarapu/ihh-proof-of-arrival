  3. 3 A player-piano roll

    This paper roll is the secret key. The inventors borrowed the idea from player pianos: a piano roll may have 88 rows of holes, so their system could use 88 different frequencies.

    Source
    • “we contemplate employing records of the type used for many years in player pianos” — First page of the patent's text (page 1 of the specification)
    • “In a conventional player piano record there may be 88 rows of perforations, and in our system such a record would permit the use of 88 different carrier frequencies” — First page of the patent's text (page 1 of the specification)

  4. 4 The receiver

    Figure 2 is the receiving side. A matching roll changes its tuning at the same moments, so only a receiver with the same record can follow the signal.

    Source
    • “Fig. 2 is a schematic diagram of the apparatus at a receiving station;” — First page of the patent's text (page 1 of the specification)
    • “employs a pair of synchronous records, one at the transmitting station and one at the receiving station, which change the tuning of the transmitting and receiving apparatus from time to time” — First page of the patent's text (page 1 of the specification)
    • “without knowledge of the records an enemy would be unable to determine at what frequency a controlling impulse would be sent” — First page of the patent's text (page 1 of the specification)

  5. 5 Ship and torpedo

    Figure 3 starts the motors at both ends at the same moment: the transmitting station and the torpedo. Without the records, an enemy could not tell which frequency the next command would use.
