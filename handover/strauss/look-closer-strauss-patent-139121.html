  2. 2 The rivets

    Each small b is a metal rivet at the edge of a pocket opening. The seams there usually ripped when hands were pushed into the pockets; a rivet binds the cloth together.

    Source
    • “b b represent the rivets at each edge of the pocket opening.” — The patent's specification (PDF page 2 of 2)
    • “The seams are usually ripped or started by the placing of the hands in the pockets and the consequent pressure or strain upon them.” — The patent's specification (PDF page 2 of 2)

  3. 3 Side seam A

    A is the side seam, which ends at the pocket. That end is exactly where the rivet goes.

    Source
    • “A is the side seam in a pair of pants, drawers, or other article of wearing apparel, which terminates at the pockets” — The patent's specification (PDF page 2 of 2)

  4. 4 How it is fixed

    Each rivet is pushed through a hole and then hammered flat on both sides, locking the two layers of cloth together.

    Source
    • “which I pass through a hole at the end of the seam, so as to bind the two parts of cloth together, and then head it down upon both sides so as to firmly unite the two parts” — The patent's specification (PDF page 2 of 2)
