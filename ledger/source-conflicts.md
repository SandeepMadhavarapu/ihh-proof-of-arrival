# Source conflicts

Conflicts we found. Most we did **not** resolve and reproduce on the public page rather than
smooth over; where the evidence does settle one, we say so and show our working. If you can
settle one still open, the page should change.

## 1. The date of the 1998 ceremony — resolved to 10 November

| Evidence | Says |
|---|---|
| Archived filename `981110_oath.html` | 10 November 1998 |
| The document's indexed page title, `11/10/98: SECRETARY ALBRIGHT COMMEMORATES…` | 10 November (US format) |
| Associated Press, carried by CBS News, timestamped 10 November 1998 2:26pm EST, reporting the ceremony as happening "Tuesday" | 10 November 1998 — which **was** a Tuesday |
| Near-universal secondary citation, including our own earlier drafts | 11 November 1998 |

**Our reading, stated as a reading:** three independent indicators agree on 10 November — the
archived filename, the indexed page title, and the Associated Press dateline — and 10 November
1998 was the Tuesday the AP report names. "11 November" looks like a day/month misreading of
"11/10/98". **We resolve the ceremony date to 10 November.** The statement itself remains
unreachable from this environment (see `fetch-attempts.txt`), so we still cannot read it — but
its date is not genuinely in doubt.

**Why it matters beyond pedantry:** 11 November 1948 is also the date commonly given for the
family's arrival. The ceremony being on 10 November means the fiftieth-anniversary framing is
approximate ("almost to the day"), and the 1948 arrival *day* — which rests only on the
unread statement — remains unestablished. That one is still open; see the Verification Desk.

## 2. How many new citizens — 86 or 97

Our earlier drafts printed **86 new citizens from 31 countries**. The Associated Press report
gives **97 new citizens from 31 countries**. The country count agrees; the people count does
not. The figure of 86 has no source we can now identify. **The 86 figure is withdrawn and appears nowhere as a claim.** Where 97 appears it is
attributed to the Associated Press, not asserted as settled.

## 3. The first-person quotation — two different wordings

Our earlier drafts quoted, as if from the State Department statement:

> "To all of you who take the oath of citizenship today, I offer my warmest congratulations.
> Today marks a new beginning in your lives. And an ongoing chapter in the story of America
> which is, above all else, the story of immigrants."

The Associated Press carries a shorter, single-sentence version with no "warmest
congratulations" opening. **The page prints the AP wording and attributes it to AP**, because
that is the one we actually read. The longer version may be the fuller statement text, or it
may be an embellishment that entered through repetition. Unresolved.

## 4. Relatives lost in the Holocaust — "more than twenty" or "more than a dozen"

Albright's own wording, quoted in a 2012 review of *Prague Winter*, is "more than twenty of my
relatives". A Jewish Telegraphic Agency report of 15 July 1997 says "more than a dozen". The
Washington Post separately establishes that all three of her surviving grandparents died in the
camps. **The page carries all three statements with their sources** and does not pick.

## 5. Her maternal grandmother's fate

One account: arrested 1942, sent to Theresienstadt and then to a destination in Poland,
"**perhaps Trawniki**". The JTA report of 1997: her fate is simply unknown. The Post explains
why — the relevant records "show that several different Anna Spieglovas died in the camps".
**The hedge is in the source and stays in the page.** It is not ours to resolve.

## 6. Einstein's doctorate — and a correction to ourselves

`nobelprize.org` states "In 1905 he obtained his doctor's degree." The University of Zurich,
which conferred it, states the doctorate "and corresponding certificate were conferred in
January 1906"; the American Institute of Physics gives faculty approval 27 July 1905 and the
award 15 January 1906. **An earlier version of our own exhibit "corrected" the museum's 1906 to
1905 on the Nobel biography's authority. That correction was wrong** and is withdrawn on the
page under its own heading.

## 7. Einstein's ETH diploma — 1900 or 1901

ETH Zurich dates the leaving certificate `Abgangszeugnis, 28.07.1900`. The Nobel biography says
"In 1901, the year he gained his diploma". 1901 is the year he was granted Swiss citizenship.
We have not established which is the slip. Both are a decade from the museum's "1911", which is
the error the page actually relies on.
