The Verification Desk

Examine the claim. Open the evidence. Record your finding, then say which piece of evidence settled it. There is no timer, no score and no way to lose — you can change any verdict at any time, and the curator’s reasoning appears whether you agree with it or not.

This is a corrections log, and it is not a one-way audit. Some claims were found on published inductee pages; 6 are this exhibit’s own, and 2 of those did not hold up either — including one where the museum was right and this exhibit was wrong, and one where we had been quoting a document nobody on this project had ever opened. They are on the desk under their own names. Published claims were read on 2 October 2026 and re-checked on 30 October 2026.

Why a museum publishes its corrections

Every museum makes errors, and the ones with the most authority are the ones that publish their own corrections — which is why this desk includes the mistakes we made ourselves while building it.

A case file is a list of claims and a list of sources. The claims can come from anywhere — an inductee page, a textbook, a newspaper, a family story, a chatbot, or an earlier draft of this exhibit. Each correction names the evidence that settled it, so you can check it and disagree with us.

Case file — arrived 1933 from Ulm, Germany

Albert Einstein

The case file where the museum turns out to be right and the famous source wrong. Read the second and third claims together.

  1. Claim as published

    Claim 1 of 4

    “Albert Einstein immigrated to Princeton, N.J., in 1932.” The same year appears twice on the page.

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Paraphrase The Institute for Advanced Study, on its own opening day

      The Institute opened on 2 October 1933. Its account of that day records that Einstein and Hermann Weyl “were in Europe and would arrive later in the month.” He was therefore not in Princeton in 1932, and took up the appointment in October 1933.

      Institute for Advanced Study, “Opening Day, October 2, 1933”

    2. Our inference Where a 1932 date can come from

      Einstein visited the United States before 1933, including a period at Caltech. A visit is not an immigration. This is our reading of how the two get confused; it is not a claim made by any source.

      Our inference, labelled as such

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    1933. The error appears twice on one page, which means it exists in two fields and was not simply mistyped once.

    What this one teaches. An error in two places is a copying error. Fixing one instance leaves the other.

  2. Claim as published

    Claim 2 of 4

    “Eidgenössische Polytechnische Schule (1911)”

    Found in the education field of a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Record ETH Zurich holds the document and dates it

      ETH Zurich’s library states that “In July 1900 he completed his studies at the Polytechnic Institute with a diploma,” and offers the leaving certificate itself for download, labelled Abgangszeugnis, 28.07.1900.

      ETH Zurich Library, “Einstein’s Studies at the Polytechnic Institute in Zurich (1896–1900)”

    2. Quotation What the Nobel Prize biography says instead

      “In 1901, the year he gained his diploma…” — 1901 is the year Einstein was granted Swiss citizenship. The awarding institution and the Nobel biography do not agree, and we have not resolved which is the slip.

      NobelPrize.org, “Albert Einstein – Biographical”

    3. Our inference Either way, not 1911

      In 1911 Einstein was a full professor at the German University in Prague. Whichever of 1900 or 1901 is right, 1911 is a decade out.

      Our inference from the two records above

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    The diploma is 1900 by the record of the school that issued it. This is the genuine error on the page, and it is a decade wide.

    What this one teaches. When two reputable sources disagree, prefer the one that performed the act. ETH issued the certificate; the Nobel Foundation did not.

  3. Claim as published

    Claim 3 of 4

    “Ph.D in Physics from University of Zurich (1906)”

    Found in the education field of a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Quotation The University of Zurich, on its own conferral

      “The 26-year-old Einstein completed his 17-page dissertation, entitled ‘A New Determination of Molecular Dimensions’, on 30 April 1905” and “submitted it to the University of Zurich almost two months later… His doctorate and corresponding certificate were conferred in January 1906.”

      University of Zurich, UZH News, “Doctor Einstein” (2022)

    2. Paraphrase Three dates, not one

      Dissertation completed 30 April 1905. Approved unanimously by the Philosophy II faculty 27 July 1905. “The degree was formally awarded January 15, 1906.”

      American Institute of Physics, Center for History of Physics, Einstein chronology 1905

    3. Quotation What the Nobel Prize biography says instead

      “In 1905 he obtained his doctor’s degree.”

      NobelPrize.org, “Albert Einstein – Biographical”

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    The museum is right and this exhibit was wrong. An earlier version of this page marked 1906 as an error and gave 1905 as the correction, on the authority of the Nobel biography. The University of Zurich conferred the degree on 15 January 1906. There are three separable events here — the dissertation completed in April 1905, the faculty approval in July 1905, and the conferral in January 1906 — and flattening them is what produces the disagreement.

    What this one teaches. Check the correction as hard as the claim. This one was wrong, and it was wrong because a famous source was trusted over the institution that did the thing.

  4. Claim as published

    Claim 4 of 4

    Awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics.

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence1 item

    1. Record The prize record

      The Nobel Prize in Physics 1921 was awarded to Albert Einstein, with the citation naming his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect — not relativity. The 1921 prize was reserved and announced in 1922.

      NobelPrize.org, The Nobel Prize in Physics 1921

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    Correct. The citation itself is the interesting part and most people have it wrong.

    What this one teaches. Confirming a claim is often where the good detail is, not where the work stops.

Case file — arrived 1908 from Eltham, London, England

Bob Hope

Four claims. The first is the one worth the time.

  1. Claim as published

    Claim 1 of 4

    “Won an Academy Award 5 times (1941, 1945, 1953, 1960, 1966)”

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence4 items

    1. Record What the Academy's own lists show

      The Academy lists Bob Hope among recipients of the Honorary Award for 1952 (25th) and 1965 (38th), and of the Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award for 1959 (32nd). These are the three we confirmed at the Academy itself.

      Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences — Honorary Award and Jean Hersholt Humanitarian Award recipient lists

    2. Quotation The 1965 citation, in the Academy's words

      “To Bob Hope for unique and distinguished service to our industry and the Academy” — presented by Jack Lemmon, 18 April 1966.

      Academy Awards Acceptance Speech Database, entry 038-28

    3. Unresolved Two further honours we could not confirm at the Academy

      A special silver plaque (commonly dated 1939/12th) and a Life Membership in the Academy (commonly dated 1944/17th) are widely reported. The Academy Awards Database renders entirely in the browser and could not be queried here, and the relevant ceremony pages returned incomplete lists. We did not confirm these two.

      Attempted: awardsdatabase.oscars.org and oscars.org ceremony pages

    4. Our inference Why the page's five years do not line up with the Academy's

      The Academy names an honour for the award year; the ceremony happens the following spring. Four of the page's five years sit exactly one year after a confirmed or reported honour (1952→1953, 1959→1960, 1965→1966, and 1944→1945). The fifth does not: 1941 is two years after the commonly reported 1939 special award. So the ceremony-year explanation accounts for most of the gap and not all of it, and we are not going to pretend otherwise.

      Our inference, and its limit, from the Academy's own numbering

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    An Honorary Award, a humanitarian award and a life membership are given outside the competitive categories; they are not won against nominees. Whatever the final count, the page describes a kind of winning that did not happen.

    What this one teaches. Check the noun before the number. “Won an Academy Award” and “received an Academy honour” are different claims.

  2. This exhibit’s own claim

    Claim 2 of 4

    “He was never nominated in a competitive Academy Award category.” — a claim this exhibit itself made in an earlier version.

    Our own earlier text, withdrawn here.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Our inference Why we withdrew it

      No competitive nomination for Bob Hope appears in the Academy honour lists we consulted. But those lists are lists of honours; absence from them is not evidence of absence from the nomination record.

      Our reasoning

    2. Unresolved What would actually establish it

      A null result from a search of the Academy Awards Database for “Hope, Bob” returning only honorary and special entries, recorded with the date the search was run. That database is client-side rendered and we could not query it from here.

      Method stated so somebody can finish it

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Not supported

    We believe the claim is true. We cannot show you that it is. Those are different states and an exhibit that conflates them has no business correcting anyone.

    What this one teaches. Proving a negative needs a named register and a search date, not an absence of contrary memories.

  3. Claim as published

    Claim 3 of 4

    The structured field reads “Ellis Island, 1908” and “Citizenship Date: 1920”, while the prose on the same page reads “Bob Hope immigrated to Cleveland, Ohio, in 1920.”

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Record Arrival and citizenship

      The family relocated to Cleveland in March 1908. Hope became a United States citizen in December 1920 — derivatively, when his father was naturalised, which is the ordinary route for a child.

      Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, Case Western Reserve University, “Hope, Leslie Townes (Bob)”

    2. Our inference The page has both numbers and uses them for the same event

      1908 is the arrival. 1920 is the citizenship, and the page’s own structured field says so. The prose then uses 1920 as the immigration year.

      Our reading of the two statements

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    The page holds the right answer in one field and contradicts it one paragraph later. Both statements cannot describe an arrival.

    What this one teaches. When a page disagrees with itself, the error is proven without leaving the page.

  4. Claim as published

    Claim 4 of 4

    Born in Eltham, England, in 1903.

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence1 item

    1. Record Birth

      “HOPE, LESLIE TOWNES (BOB)”, born 29 May 1903 in Eltham, England, a London suburb.

      Encyclopedia of Cleveland History, Case Western Reserve University

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    Correct and specific enough to check.

    What this one teaches. Most of what the site says is fine. A verifier who finds fault everywhere is as useless as one who finds none.

Case file — arrived 1849 from Dunbar, Scotland

John Muir

Five claims. By the fourth you should stop checking facts and start auditing the process.

  1. Claim as published

    Claim 1 of 5

    “Helped establish … Grand Canyon (1908) National Park.”

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Record Monument, 1908

      Grand Canyon was proclaimed a National Monument on 11 January 1908 under the Antiquities Act, by Proclamation No. 794.

      National Park Service, Ronald F. Lee, “The Proclamation of National Monuments Under the Antiquities Act, 1906–1970”, Table II

    2. Record Park, 1919

      “Designated as Grand Canyon National Park by an act of Congress on February 26” (40 Stat. 1175), 1919.

      National Park Service, Grand Canyon National Park Profile 2025, park chronology

    3. Record Muir died in 1914

      The Sierra Club gives his dates as 1838–1914. We deliberately do not assert a day: no institutional source we opened states one. The park was established on 26 February 1919, so the gap is at least four years and two months on any reading of his death date.

      Sierra Club, John Muir Exhibit

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    Two errors stacked. A monument is not a park, and the park postdates his death by more than four years on any reading.

    What this one teaches. Check the date against the person’s lifespan. It is the cheapest test there is.

  2. Claim as published

    Claim 2 of 5

    “Yosemite (1898).”

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Quotation The National Park Service

      “Established as a national park on October 1, 1890…”

      National Park Service, NPS Geodiversity Atlas — Yosemite National Park

    2. Our inference The right answer is already on the page

      The same sentence dates Sequoia correctly to 1890. The correct year is printed one clause from the wrong one.

      Our reading of the page

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    1 October 1890.

    What this one teaches. Errors cluster. Where you find one, read the whole sentence again slowly.

  3. Claim as published

    Claim 3 of 5

    “The John Muir Trust was formed in England…”

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Record The Trust's own registration details

      Scotland. Founded 1983. Scottish Charity No. SC002061, Company No. SC081620, registered office Tower House, Station Road, Pitlochry, PH16 5AN.

      John Muir Trust, About (organisational details)

    2. Unresolved Not independently cross-checked

      These are the Trust’s own statements about itself. The Scottish Charity Regulator’s register renders in the browser and we could not query it here.

      Attempted: OSCR register

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    Scotland. On a page about a Scot, this is the error a Scottish visitor notices first.

    What this one teaches. Nationality errors land hardest on the people the page is about.

  4. Claim as published

    Claim 4 of 5

    “Information sources: Britannica, Biography, Grammys.”

    Found at the foot of a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Record The same line, on a different person's page

      The identical string appears at the foot of the Celia Cruz page. Cruz was a Grammy-winning singer. Muir was a naturalist who died in 1914, decades before the Grammy Awards existed. Both pages were read on 2 October 2026.

      immigranthallofhonor.org, John Muir and Celia Cruz inductee pages

    2. Our inference What this means for the rest of the page

      A source list that names an award body with no connection to the subject is not describing where the information came from. We do not know how this happened and we do not speculate.

      Our reasoning

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Not supported

    The source list is not functioning as a source list. That matters more than any single wrong date, because it is the thing a reader would use to check the rest.

    What this one teaches. When the sourcing fails, stop verifying individual facts and audit the process.

  5. Claim as published

    Claim 5 of 5

    Born in Dunbar, Scotland, in 1838; co-founded the Sierra Club.

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence1 item

    1. Quotation The Sierra Club, on its own founding

      “The Sierra Club was organized in 1892…” and “John Muir was the Sierra Club’s first President and held that office for twenty-two years – until his death.”

      Sierra Club, “John Muir, President of the Sierra Club”

Case file — arrived 1961 from Havana, Cuba

Celia Cruz

A bill mistaken for an award, and two numbers nobody can source.

  1. Claim as published

    Claim 1 of 3

    “Awarded the Congressional Gold Medal (2005).”

    Found on a published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Record H.R. 149, 109th Congress — the full legislative history

      The Congressional Tribute to Celia Cruz Act was introduced on 4 January 2005 by Rep. Menendez and referred to the Committee on Financial Services the same day. On 23 February 2005 it was referred to the Subcommittee on Domestic and International Monetary Policy, Trade and Technology. That is the latest action. It did not become law.

      govinfo.gov, official bill status record BILLSTATUS-109hr149.xml

    2. Record She appears nowhere in the list of recipients

      The Congressional Research Service’s chronological list of every Congressional Gold Medal recipient contains no entry for Cruz. The edition consulted runs to 2016.

      Congressional Research Service RL30076, “Congressional Gold Medals, 1776–2016”

    3. Record The honours she did receive

      National Medal of Arts, 1994. A US Mint American Women Quarter in 2024. Both are frequently conflated with a Congressional Gold Medal; neither is one.

      National Endowment for the Arts, National Medal of Arts — Celia Cruz

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Contradicted

    A bill introduced is not an award conferred. The medal requires an Act of Congress, and this bill died in subcommittee.

    What this one teaches. Introduced, reported, passed, enacted and awarded are five different things.

  2. Claim as published

    Claim 2 of 3

    “She recorded more than 70 albums” in the prose, and “Produced 70 songs” in the achievements list. (The “23 gold records” in the same list is corroborated by her estate and is not part of this claim.)

    Found: both statements on the same published inductee page. Read 2 October 2026.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Quotation The Smithsonian

      “Cruz recorded more than 80 albums and songs” — note that the Smithsonian’s own phrasing combines the two categories rather than giving a clean album count.

      Smithsonian National Museum of American History, “Celia Cruz, Queen of Salsa”

    2. Paraphrase Her estate

      “Celia recorded over 80 albums and 1,000 songs, earned 23 Gold Records, and won five Grammy Awards.”

      celiacruz.com, Biography

    3. Our inference Why this is 'not supported' rather than 'contradicted'

      We cannot show that 70 is wrong, because no source we opened gives a definitive count and the categories differ between them. We can show that 70 is not what the two best sources say, and that the page uses the same number for two different things.

      Our reasoning

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Not supported

    Albums are not songs, and 70 is not the figure either the Smithsonian or her estate gives. An earlier version of this exhibit called this “contradicted” and endorsed the album figure. That overstated what we could show.

    What this one teaches. “Not supported” is a real verdict, and usually the honest one.

  3. This exhibit’s own claim

    Claim 3 of 3

    She left Cuba in 1960 and arrived in the United States on 20 November 1961.

    This exhibit’s own claim.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Quotation The Smithsonian

      “In 1960, Cruz went on tour to Mexico with the orchestra. She never returned to Cuba,” and she arrived in the United States “on Nov. 20, 1961.”

      Smithsonian National Museum of American History, “Celia Cruz, Queen of Salsa”

    2. Unresolved What we removed

      An earlier version said she was naturalised in 1961. No source we opened supports a naturalisation year, including her estate’s own biography, which says only that she moved to the United States. The claim is withdrawn rather than softened.

      Recorded as a withdrawal

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    Leaving and arriving are two different dates for one journey — which is exactly the distinction the Bob Hope page loses.

    What this one teaches. One journey, several dates, and a naturalisation that is none of them.

Case file — arrived 1948 from Prague, Czechoslovakia

Madeleine Albright

The case file where the honest answer is that we have not read the document.

  1. This exhibit’s own claim

    Claim 1 of 4

    “She arrived in the United States on 11 November 1948” — and the State Department press statement said so.

    This exhibit’s own claim, in an earlier version.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Not read at source The primary document, which nobody on this project has opened

      US Department of State, Office of the Spokesman, press statement, archived at 1997-2001.state.gov/albright/981110_oath.html. This host is refused by our network on every route, including web archives. An earlier version of this exhibit quoted this document at length without reading it. Every such quotation has now been withdrawn across the whole package.

      Attempted repeatedly; blocked

    2. Record The statement's date, resolved to 10 November

      The filename is 981110 — 10 November. The indexed page title reads “11/10/98”, and the Associated Press carried a 10 November dateline for the ceremony. The 1998 ceremony date resolves to 10 November; it is widely miscited as 11 November. That still does not establish the 1948 arrival day, which no source we opened records.

      Filename, indexed title and the AP dateline, against the common miscitation

    3. Paraphrase What a contemporaneous wire report says

      Associated Press, carried by CBS News and timestamped 10 November 1998, reports the ceremony happening “Tuesday”. 10 November 1998 was a Tuesday. The AP report gives 97 new citizens from 31 countries — not the 86 this exhibit previously printed.

      CBS News / Associated Press, “Albright Renews Her Oath”, 10 November 1998

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Not supported

    The arrival date may well be 11 November 1948. We are not able to show you that, because the document we were citing for it is one we have never opened. An exhibit built on the principle that a citation is not a check cannot itself cite something it has not read.

    What this one teaches. The failure mode this exhibit exists to teach is the one it committed. Read the document or say that you have not.

  2. This exhibit’s own claim

    Claim 2 of 4

    Her papers went to the Library of Congress, and they are not available online.

    This exhibit’s own claim.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Quotation The finding aid's acquisition note

      “The papers of Madeleine Korbel Albright, U.S. secretary of state, diplomat, and educator, were given to the Library of Congress by Albright in 2014 and 2016.” 81,000 items; 232 containers plus 2 classified and 3 oversize; 94.5 linear feet.

      Library of Congress, Manuscript Division, Madeleine Korbel Albright Papers, finding aid ms018034

    2. Quotation Access, in the Library's words

      “Restrictions apply governing the use, photoduplication, or publication of items in this collection… Access to digital content is available onsite only in the Manuscript Reading Room and requires advanced notice.” Not digitised at item level.

      Same finding aid, restrictions note

    3. Our inference Where 'acquired 2015' comes from

      The Library announced the donation in a press release dated 16 September 2015. 2015 is the announcement year; 2014 and 2016 are the gift years of record. An earlier version of our own research note said 2015.

      Our correction of our own earlier text

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    The collection exists, it is large, and none of it is online. That is why this profile is built from published federal records rather than from her papers.

    What this one teaches. An announcement date is not an acquisition date. Use the finding aid’s own words.

  3. This exhibit’s own claim

    Claim 3 of 4

    She was the first woman to serve as United States Secretary of State.

    This exhibit’s own claim.

    Open the evidence2 items

    1. Record The Office of the Historian

      Sworn in 23 January 1997 as the 64th Secretary of State, the first woman to hold the office. The same entry renders her birth name “Marie Jean ‘Madlenka’ Korbel”, born 15 May 1937 in Prague.

      US Department of State, Office of the Historian, Madeleine Korbel Albright

    2. Unresolved The name has two renderings and we verified only one

      The Czech form Marie Jana Korbelová is in common use. We did not verify it against any Czech institutional source, so it appears here as a variant, not as a correction of the State Department.

      Recorded as unverified

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    Correct, and precisely datable. Note the boundary: first woman Secretary of State, not first woman in the line of succession — as a naturalised citizen she was not eligible for the presidency.

    What this one teaches. A superlative is only true inside a stated category.

  4. This exhibit’s own claim

    Claim 4 of 4

    She learned in 1997 that her family was Jewish and that relatives had died in the Holocaust.

    This exhibit’s own claim.

    Open the evidence3 items

    1. Quotation The newspaper that told her

      The Washington Post records that she “was raised as a Roman Catholic and was never told by her parents or anyone else that family members had perished in the Holocaust,” and that she was “presented with research undertaken for this article about her family history.” Its separate finding: “all three of her surviving grandparents died in the concentration camps.”

      The Washington Post, “Madeleine Albright didn’t know she was Jewish until The Post told her”, 23 March 2022

    2. Quotation Her own figure, in her own words

      “I had no idea that my family heritage was Jewish or that more than twenty of my relatives had died in the Holocaust.” The figure of more than twenty is hers, not the newspaper’s.

      Albright, quoted in a review of Prague Winter, j. the Jewish News of Northern California, 31 August 2012

    3. Unresolved Her maternal grandmother — the hedge is in the source and stays there

      She “was arrested in 1942 and shipped first to Theresienstadt and then to a destination in Poland, perhaps Trawniki, where she was murdered.” The Post explains the uncertainty: the relevant Nazi records “show that several different Anna Spieglovas died in the camps.” A 1997 JTA wire report says flatly that her fate is unknown, and gives “more than a dozen” relatives rather than more than twenty. We print the divergence rather than choosing between them.

      j. the Jewish News of Northern California, 31 August 2012; JTA archive, 15 July 1997

    Record your finding
    Which piece of evidence settled it?
    The curator’s finding

    Confirmed

    Confirmed in substance. The discipline is in what this does not say: the hedge is reproduced, the two different relative counts are both shown with their sources, and the reporter’s name is omitted because we did not verify the byline.

    What this one teaches. Where the source hedges, you hedge. You do not resolve somebody else’s uncertainty on their behalf, least of all about how a family died.

Why a museum would build this

Every other way of making a history site “engaging” asks the visitor to trust it more. This one asks them to trust it less, and hands them the tools. The claims are real, the errors are real, and the evidence is named and linked well enough that a visitor can go and disagree with us — which, on four of these claims, would have been the right call.

It is also the cheapest thing here to extend. A new case file is a list of claims and a list of sources in cases.py — no new code, no new design, no developer.
