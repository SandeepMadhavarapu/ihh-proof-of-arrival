Share your story

If you came here from somewhere else, or your family did, we would like to keep the record of it. You decide how much to tell and what we may do with it.

This page cannot receive your story — and it should not pretend to

There was a submission form here. It has been removed, for two reasons that are worth stating plainly.

It had nowhere to send anything. This is a demonstration site with no server behind it. A form that accepts a story and silently discards it is worse than no form, because the person who wrote it believes it arrived.

It leaked personal data into the address bar. The form had no method, so the browser defaulted to GET — which put the writer’s email address and the full text of their story into the URL, their browser history, and any referrer header sent to another site. An independent reviewer demonstrated this with a test submission. For an organisation collecting immigrant family histories, some from people whose position is not secure, that is a serious defect and not a cosmetic one.

Where to actually send a story

The Immigrant Hall of Honor collects stories through its own site, and those forms work. As read on 2 October 2026 there are four, segmented by who is telling the story: first-generation immigrants, second-generation, genealogists, and a general form.

Go to the real submission form

That link leaves this demonstration and goes to the live Immigrant Hall of Honor website. We have no control over what it does with what you send.

The terms they ask you to agree to

Three of the four live forms carry the same clause, quoted here as it appeared on 2 October 2026 so you can compare it with whatever you are shown:

I retain ownership of my copyright and grant the Project a non-exclusive, royalty-free license to reproduce, publish, display, distribute, and otherwise use my story or interview, in whole or in part, for historical, educational, archival, museum, outreach, promotional, fundraising and research purposes.

That is a good clause. You keep your copyright and you are lending it, not signing it away. An earlier version of this audit criticised IHH for running two contradictory copyright regimes; they have since harmonised them, and that criticism is withdrawn.

One narrower point survives. The second-generation form takes no position on copyright at all, and asks you to confirm you have your relative’s permission to share their story. That confirmation is not verifiable by anyone, and the story being shared belongs to somebody who is not filling in the form.

Quotations above were read through an automated fetch on 2 October 2026. Check the wording on the form itself before relying on it.
