Rights and reuse

What we publish, where it came from, what you may do with it, and how to tell us we have got something wrong.

Our standard

Every catalogued image and document carries its holding institution and a link to that institution’s own record. Where the institution states a rights position we reproduce it word for word: the Albright handoff’s media manifest records one for all fourteen items, and the worked examples on this site show the full rights panel in place. We do not paraphrase a rights statement, and we do not write “public domain” unless the institution used those words.

Several distinctions matter and are easy to get wrong, so we state them plainly:

  • “No known restrictions on publication” is not a public-domain determination. It is a statement about what the holding institution knows. The Library of Congress says so itself: it generally does not own the rights to material in its collections, and the obligation to clear a work rests with the user.
  • A work appearing on a government website is not automatically a government work. 17 U.S.C. §105 removes copyright from works made by federal employees in the course of their duties — not from everything a federal agency happens to host.
  • Metadata and image can carry different licences. Several museums release catalogue metadata under CC0 while reserving rights in the photograph of the object. A CC0 badge on a record does not mean the picture is free.
  • A download button is not a licence.

What you may do with material we publish

Where an item’s rights panel shows an institutional statement permitting reuse, follow that statement — it governs, not us. Where we hold the rights ourselves, you may reuse our written text for non-commercial educational purposes with attribution to the Immigrant Hall of Honor and a link back.

Contributed family stories belong to the people who wrote them. We hold a non-exclusive licence to publish them; we do not own them and cannot license them onward to you.

If we have got something wrong

If you hold rights in something we have published, or you believe a credit or a fact is wrong, write to us and we will correct or remove it. We would rather be told than be right. Include the page, the item, and what you believe the correct position is.

What we ask of contributors

We ask contributors to confirm they have the right to share what they upload. Because we ask that of them, we hold ourselves to the same standard in public, on every page. That is the whole reason this page exists.
