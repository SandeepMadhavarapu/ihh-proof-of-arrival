Press Statement by James P. Rubin, Spokesman
November 11, 1998
Secretary Albright Commemorates 50 Years in the United States

Celebrating the 50th anniversary of her arrival in the United States, Secretary of State Madeleine Korbel Albright, along with her sister, renewed her Oath of Allegiance at a naturalization ceremony for nearly 100 new American citizens. Immediately following the administration of the Oath by Judge Paul L. Friedman of the US District Court for the District of Columbia, Secretary Albright led the group of new citizens in the Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag.

"To all of you who take the oath of citizenship today, I offer my warmest congratulations. Today marks a new beginning in your lives. And an ongoing chapter in the story of America which is, above all else, the story of immigrants," Secretary Albright remarked earlier in the ceremony.

Reflecting on her family's arrival in New York on November 11, 1948, Secretary Albright said: "... this nation was generous enough to welcome us, make us feel at home, and allow me to grow up in freedom. For that, I will always be grateful."

Secretary Albright's family, the Korbels, arrived in the United States from Czechoslovakia November 11, 1948 when the Secretary was 11 years old. The Korbel family fled the Communist takeover of Czechoslovakia. The Korbels arrived in New York and settled in Denver, Colorado. This was the second time the Korbels were forced from their homeland, having fled to England when the Nazis invaded during World War II.

The ceremony today naturalized 86 new citizens who came to the United States from 31 countries. Secretary Albright recognized three individuals with whom she had met prior to the ceremony. Among them was Arlinda Kaba, who came from Albania, where her relatives were imprisoned under the old regime for more than a decade. She came to the US as a student and now works as an epidemiologist at the Centers for Disease Control. The Secretary also spoke of Caridad Sahagun, who was born in Cuba and came to the United States in 1980. She was eager to become an American citizen because she was denied freedom in the repression of Castro's Cuba and now she will be able to vote in a free election for the first time in her life. And finally, Secretary Albright mentioned Feleke Kassa, who was forced to flee the Marxist dictatorship in Ethiopia. Like many refugees, Mr. Kassa is working hard at two jobs in order to support his family in the US and help his relatives still in Ethiopia.

Immigration and Naturalization Service Commissioner Doris Meissner said about the ceremony, "I offer my very best wishes to Secretary of State Madeleine Albright on the 50th anniversary of her arrival to the United States and applaud her for celebrating the occasion by honoring our nation's newest citizens. Secretary Albright is a shining example of how our country's greatness is deeply rooted in our rich immigration tradition. I join her today in congratulating our new citizens who, with their unique talents and contributions, will strengthen this great heritage."
